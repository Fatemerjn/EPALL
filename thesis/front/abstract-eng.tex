\begin{latin}

	\begin{center}
		\textbf{Abstract}
	\end{center}
	\baselineskip=.8\baselineskip

	Continual learning systems deployed in real-world environments must often honor requests to unlearn specific information. In task-incremental settings, selective forgetting requires suppressing a designated target task while maintaining performance on all retained tasks. The central obstacle is \emph{parameter overlap}: when tasks share underlying model parameters, resetting or repairing weights for one task frequently degrades the others. The Privacy-Aware Lifelong Learning (PALL) framework modularizes task knowledge through stored subnetworks, but its request-time reset and repair do not distinguish shared coordinates by their sensitivity to the retained tasks.

	This thesis introduces EPALL, an enhanced PALL procedure that identifies the shared support between the target subnetwork and the union of the active subnetworks, ranks those coordinates by retained-task gradient magnitude, and anchors the high-sensitivity subset with an $\ell_2$ penalty during the repair phase. In addition, an exploratory parameter-efficient pathway, PALL-Adapter, is designed and audited through matched component controls.

	On Split-CIFAR benchmarks, an eleven-method five-seed comparison shows that EPALL drives forgotten-task accuracy to the chance level ($0.4942$ on CIFAR-10 and $0.0952$ on CIFAR-100) while staying within half a percentage point of the full-isolation CLPU reference in retained accuracy. Against the original PALL algorithm, paired analysis favours EPALL in all five CIFAR-100 seeds (exact one-sided $p=0.03125$), and WorstDrop falls from $0.0264$ to $0.0052$. A controlled $120$-run benchmark over five pre-specified overlap levels shows that PALL-Original's WorstDrop grows with overlap while EPALL stays comparatively flat, and resident-state accounting shows CLPU using $1.6$--$2.2\times$ as much accounted state. Conversely, matched component controls attribute PALL-Adapter's task suppression primarily to target-path reset rather than to its overlap-aware soft mask; this negative result is reported explicitly. No result establishes exact, certified, or storage-level removal, and all claims are empirical.

	\bigskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords}:

	Continual Learning, Machine Unlearning, Selective Forgetting, Parameter Overlap, Protected Subnetworks, Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning

\end{latin}
