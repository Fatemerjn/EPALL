\begin{abstract}
Selective forgetting asks a continual learner to drop a requested task without hurting the tasks that stay active, which is hardest exactly when those tasks share weights. We build two overlap-aware variants of Privacy-Aware Lifelong Learning (PALL). \textbf{PALL-Modified} locates the shared support of the forgotten and retained tasks, ranks the coordinates that matter most to retention, and anchors them during replay-based repair. \textbf{PALL-Adapter} freezes the backbone and splits computation into task-specific and shared bottleneck adapters; forget and retained gradients then drive a soft-masked update of the shared adapter, followed by classifier unlearning and a short retained-task repair. For that shared-adapter step we prove that the soft mask shrinks its first-order interference on the critical overlap by the protection factor. In a matched Split-CIFAR comparison CLPU is the strongest baseline, but it buys isolation by storing a full network per active task; among shared-model methods, PALL-Modified stays within $0.5$ points of CLPU's final accuracy while forcing forgotten-task accuracy to chance. PALL-Adapter cuts the nominal retained-repair scope by up to $87.5\%$, and a pretrained backbone removes its from-scratch accuracy bottleneck. Membership inference, an attack-threshold leakage audit, and linear probes keep the claim where the evidence puts it: approximate task-level forgetting, not exact, certified, or storage-level data removal.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Continual learning (CL) systems acquire tasks sequentially while trying to prevent catastrophic forgetting \cite{mccloskey1989catastrophic,kirkpatrick2017overcoming}. Selective forgetting poses the inverse operational problem: after a task-level deletion request, the model should suppress that task without catastrophically degrading the tasks that remain active \cite{cao2015towards,bourtoule2021machine}. We study this task-incremental setting and do not equate task suppression with deleting individual records, raw data, checkpoints, or external copies.

The core difficulty is \emph{parameter overlap}: a high-capacity network spreads each task's knowledge across many shared weights. Running an unconstrained unlearning step on a target task $D_f$---gradient ascent, or Fisher-based scrubbing \cite{golatkar2020forgetting}---therefore also moves weights that the retained tasks $D_r$ depend on, and their accuracy drops as a side effect.

The original Privacy-Aware Lifelong Learning (PALL) framework \cite{ozdenizci2025pall} modularizes task knowledge with stored subnetworks. Its request-time reset and retained-task repair, however, do not distinguish shared coordinates by their sensitivity to retained tasks. This matters under heavy overlap, where perturbing a small but important shared region can damage multiple active tasks.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We make three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{PALL-Modified:} it measures the \emph{critical overlap} between the forgotten and the active parameters and protects it with an anchored penalty during retention repair.
    \item \textbf{PALL-Adapter:} a parameter-efficient design that freezes the backbone and routes learning through task-specific bottleneck adapters and one shared adapter, so a forget request can be confined to a small module.
    \item \textbf{Ordered overlap-aware forgetting, with analysis:} a request-time procedure that estimates the forget and retained gradients before reset, soft-masks the shared-adapter update, unlearns the classifier, deletes the target rehearsal entry, and repairs the rest. We prove that the soft mask contracts this step's first-order interference by the protection factor, and measure end-to-end stability with WorstDrop rather than claiming exact or certified removal.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Continual Learning and Memory Replay}
Continual learning accumulates knowledge across a stream of tasks \cite{thrun1998lifelong}. Regularization methods such as Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) \cite{kirkpatrick2017overcoming} and Learning without Forgetting (LwF) \cite{li2017learning} anchor parameters, while Experience Replay (ER) and Dark Experience Replay (DER++) \cite{buzzega2020dark} keep an episodic memory buffer. CLPU \cite{liu2022clpu} is the strongest isolation-based reference here: in the implementation we evaluate, each active task gets its own full network and a request is served by deleting that network. Interference is then impossible by construction, but storage grows with the number of active tasks. PALL targets the complementary case, where tasks already share one model and cannot be separated by discarding a task network.

\subsection{Machine Unlearning Strategies}
Retraining-based methods such as SISA \cite{bourtoule2021machine} partition the data into isolated ensemble members. Approximate methods instead edit gradients to scrub information \cite{golatkar2020forgetting}, and Boundary Unlearning shifts output decision boundaries for speed \cite{chen2023boundary}. In a continual setting, though, an unconstrained update can damage feature space that active tasks still use. Saliency Unlearning (SalUn) \cite{fan2024salun} confines updates to influential weights; Selective Synaptic Dampening (SSD) \cite{foster2024ssd} dampens parameters by Fisher-based importance.

\subsection{Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning}
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT), through adapters \cite{houlsby2019parameter} and LoRA \cite{hu2021lora}, restricts training to compact modules inserted into a frozen network. The recent BID-LoRA preprint \cite{rachapudi2026bidlora} explores a related continual-learning/unlearning combination. PALL-Adapter instead measures overlap inside a shared bottleneck and controls how that bottleneck moves at request time.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{The Limitation of Unconstrained Forgetting}
PALL identifies task-specific parameters, resets or reconstructs coordinates from stored masks, and repairs the retained tasks. What it does not do is separate shared coordinates by how sensitive the retained tasks are to them. Under heavy overlap, reset and repair can therefore disturb pathways that several active tasks lean on. We map that intersection and protect it directly.

\subsection{Proposed Method 1: PALL-Modified}
Let $M_f$ be the binary subnetwork mask stored for the task requested for deletion and let $M_r$ be the union of the masks of the retained tasks. Their shared support is
\begin{equation}
S_{share}=M_f\cap M_r.
\end{equation}
PALL-Modified ranks coordinates inside $S_{share}$ by retained-buffer gradient magnitude (or, in an ablation, gradient-conflict energy) and selects a critical subset $S_{share\_crit}$. The target rehearsal entry is used only during request-time diagnostics and is then removed from the in-process rehearsal memory; the method does not claim deletion of raw data, checkpoints, or other external copies. The subsequent reset is inherited from PALL and is branch-dependent because retained task-specific backups and historical fine-tuning masks determine which base coordinates must be preserved or reconstructed. Retention repair is then restricted to the nominal shared repair mask and minimizes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:repair}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{repair} ={}& \alpha\,\mathcal{L}_{DER} + \beta\,\mathcal{L}_{CE}\\
&+ \lambda \sum_{w \in S_{share\_crit}} \big\| w - w_{anchor} \big\|^2 .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal{L}_{DER}$ matches stored logits, $\mathcal{L}_{CE}$ uses retained labels, and the final term limits drift on the selected critical shared coordinates. The default anchor is the pre-request value $w_{old}$; we separately evaluate a fresh reinitialization anchor $w_{reinit}$ because $w_{old}$ can itself encode information about the forgotten task.

\subsection{Proposed Method 2: PALL-Adapter}
To cut the update footprint and keep tasks from interfering, we freeze the feature-extraction layers ($\theta_{base}$). Only three groups stay trainable:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Shared bottleneck adapter} ($\phi_{shared}$),
    \item \textbf{Task-specific adapters} ($\phi_{t}$),
    \item \textbf{Task-specific classifier heads.}
\end{enumerate}

For an input activation $x$, a residual block with ReLU \cite{nair2010relu} computes
\begin{align}
z_{shared} &= x + \mathbf{W}_{up}^{(s)} \cdot \mathrm{ReLU}\left(\mathbf{W}_{down}^{(s)} \cdot x\right), \\
z_{final} &= z_{shared} + \mathbf{W}_{up}^{(t)} \cdot \mathrm{ReLU}\left(\mathbf{W}_{down}^{(t)} \cdot z_{shared}\right).
\end{align}

\subsection{Ordered Soft-Masked Unlearning}
The implementation uses a strict order so that every forget-dependent gradient is estimated while the target rehearsal entry is still available. First, absolute shared-adapter gradients on the target and retained buffers define top-ratio sets $S_{forget}$ and $S_{active}$ and their intersection $S_{share\_crit}$. A top-ranked subset $H\subseteq S_{share\_crit}$ is hard protected. Second, the target task adapter is reinitialized (Kaiming down-projection and zero up-projection) and its classifier slice is reset. The shared adapter is then optimized toward a uniform target:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{unlearn} = \mathbb{E}_{(x,y) \in D_{forget}} \Big[ -\sum_{c} \tfrac{1}{|C|} \log p(y=c\,|\,x; \Phi) \Big].
\end{equation}
Each coordinate's update is then scaled by a \emph{soft-scaling} multiplier $m_{soft}$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:softupdate}
w^{(i+1)} = w^{(i)} - \eta \cdot m_{soft}(w) \cdot \nabla_{w} \mathcal{L}_{unlearn},
\end{equation}
where $m_{soft}(w)$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:softmask}
m_{soft}(w) = \begin{cases}
0, & \text{if } w \in H, \\
(1-p), & \text{if } w \in S_{share\_crit}\setminus H, \\
1, & \text{if } w \in S_{forget}\setminus S_{active}, \\
0.0, & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $p\in[0,1]$. Third, the target classifier receives a gradient-ascent update computed from its pre-reset forget gradient, the target rehearsal entry is deleted, and all trainable adapter and classifier parameters are briefly repaired on retained-task buffers. Thus the soft mask governs the uniform-target shared-adapter subphase; it is not a permanent protection mask over the entire request.

\begin{algorithm}[tb]
\caption{Ordered PALL-Adapter Forgetting}
\label{alg:unlearning}
\textbf{Input}: target task $t_{forget}$, active tasks $\mathcal{T}_{active}$, shared adapter $\phi_{shared}$, task adapter $\phi_{t_{forget}}$, classifier head $C_{t_{forget}}$\\
\textbf{Parameter}: $\alpha_f = 0.3$, $\alpha_p = 0.2$, learning rate $\eta$, protection strength $p$
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Compute forget and retained shared-adapter gradients from their rehearsal entries
\STATE $S_{forget} \leftarrow$ top-$\alpha_f$ coords of $|\nabla \mathcal{L}_{forget}|$; $S_{active} \leftarrow$ top-$\alpha_p$ coords of $|\nabla \mathcal{L}_{active}|$
\STATE $S_{share\_crit} \leftarrow S_{forget} \cap S_{active}$; \quad $S_{forget\_only} \leftarrow S_{forget} \setminus S_{active}$
\STATE Select hard-protected $H\subseteq S_{share\_crit}$; reset $\phi_{t_{forget}}$ and classifier slice $C_{t_{forget}}$
\FOR{iteration $= 1, \dots, N_{iters}$}
    \STATE sample batch $(x, y) \in D_{t_{forget}}$; compute $\mathcal{L}_{unlearn}$ against the uniform target
    \STATE $\nabla \phi_{shared} \leftarrow \mathrm{backward}(\mathcal{L}_{unlearn})$
    \FORALL{$w \in \phi_{shared}$}
        \STATE $m_{soft} \leftarrow 0$ on $H$; $(1-p)$ on $S_{share\_crit}\setminus H$; $1$ on $S_{forget\_only}$; else $0$
        \STATE $w \leftarrow w - \eta \cdot m_{soft} \cdot \nabla_{w}$
    \ENDFOR
\ENDFOR
\STATE Apply classifier ascent from the cached forget gradient; delete the target rehearsal entry
\STATE Repair retained tasks for $N_{repair}$ steps on their rehearsal entries
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Mechanism Scope}
The multiplier in Eq.~\eqref{eq:softmask} directly scales the shared-adapter displacement during the uniform-target subphase, but it does not by itself bound end-to-end retained accuracy: the target adapter reset, classifier ascent, and unmasked retained repair also affect the final model. We therefore treat $(1-p)$ as an implementation-level damping factor and evaluate its practical effect through measured WorstDrop. Because the final shared adapter remains a function of target-buffer gradients, neither method is an exact-unlearning or privacy-certified mechanism.

\subsection{Contraction of the Shared-Adapter Subphase}
\label{subsec:theory}
We now formalize the single component the soft mask directly controls. Consider the uniform-target subphase (Algorithm~\ref{alg:unlearning}, lines~5--10), during which $\theta_{base}$ and every task adapter are frozen and only $\phi_{shared}\in\mathbb{R}^d$ is updated by Eq.~\eqref{eq:softupdate} for $N$ steps. Let $\phi^{(0)}$ be its pre-subphase value and $\Delta_t^{\mathrm{sub}}\triangleq R_t(\phi^{(N)})-R_t(\phi^{(0)})$ the change this subphase \emph{alone} induces in a retained task's risk $R_t$; the reset, classifier ascent, and unmasked repair are separate, which is why this is not an end-to-end bound (cf.\ Mechanism Scope). Partition the coordinates into $\mathcal{F}^{\!\circ}=S_{forget}\setminus S_{active}$ ($m_i{=}1$), $\mathcal{S}=S_{share\_crit}\setminus H$ ($m_i{=}1{-}p$), and the frozen remainder ($m_i{=}0$). We assume (i) each $R_t$ is $\beta$-smooth; (ii) $|g_i^{(k)}|\le G$, so $\Gamma_i\triangleq|\sum_{k}g_i^{(k)}|\le NG$; and (iii) $|\partial_i R_t(\phi^{(0)})|\le\epsilon$ on $\mathcal{F}^{\!\circ}$---which Phase~2 enforces, since $S_{active}$ is the top-$K$ retained-gradient support.

\begin{theorem}[Critical-overlap contraction]
\label{thm:contraction}
Let $E_t^{\mathrm{crit}}=\eta\!\sum_{i\in S_{share\_crit}}\!|\partial_i R_t(\phi^{(0)})|\,\Gamma_i$ and $C_t=\eta\!\sum_{i\in\mathcal{F}^{\!\circ}}\!\Gamma_i$. Under (i)--(iii), the soft-masked subphase satisfies
\begin{equation*}
\Delta_t^{\mathrm{sub}}\!\le\!(1{-}p)E_t^{\mathrm{crit}}\!+\!\epsilon C_t\!+\!\tfrac{\beta}{2}\eta^2N^2G^2\big(|\mathcal{F}^{\!\circ}|\!+\!(1{-}p)^2|\mathcal{S}|\big),
\end{equation*}
whereas the unconstrained update ($m_i{=}1$ on all of $S_{forget}$) replaces $(1{-}p)E_t^{\mathrm{crit}}$ by $E_t^{\mathrm{crit}}$. Thus on the critical overlap the soft mask contracts the first-order subphase interference by the factor $(1{-}p)$; as $\eta NG\to0$ the curvature term vanishes and $\Delta_t^{\mathrm{sub,soft}}\le(1{-}p)\,\Delta_t^{\mathrm{sub,unc}}+o(1)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Summing Eq.~\eqref{eq:softupdate}, $\delta_i=-\eta m_i\sum_k g_i^{(k)}$, so $|\delta_i|\le\eta m_i\Gamma_i$. The descent lemma gives $\Delta_t^{\mathrm{sub}}\le\langle\nabla R_t,\delta\rangle+\tfrac{\beta}{2}\|\delta\|_2^2$; the $m_i{=}0$ coordinates vanish, $\mathcal{F}^{\!\circ}$ contributes $\le\epsilon C_t$ by~(iii), and $\mathcal{S}\subseteq S_{share\_crit}$ contributes $\le(1{-}p)E_t^{\mathrm{crit}}$. The curvature term uses $m_i^2\in\{1,(1{-}p)^2,0\}$; dropping $(1{-}p)$ recovers the unconstrained bound.
\end{proof}

\noindent This matches the logged trajectories: the measured subphase drop stays at or below the first-order prediction for three of four audited retained-task probes, the residual being uncalibrated curvature and accuracy--risk constants.

\section{Experimental Setup}

We evaluate task-level continual forgetting over three public benchmarks with fixed schedules that interleave task-training and forgetting requests. The benchmarks span a small coarse-grained problem, a larger fine-grained problem, and a higher-resolution 200-class problem, allowing us to test increasing task count and representation difficulty:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{CIFAR-10} \cite{krizhevsky2009learning}: 5 sequential tasks, 2 disjoint classes per task (random-guess forgotten-task accuracy $0.5$).
    \item \textbf{CIFAR-100} \cite{krizhevsky2009learning}: 10 sequential tasks, 5 disjoint classes per task in the overlap-heavy protocol (chance $0.2$), and 10-class tasks in the standard protocol (chance $0.1$).
    \item \textbf{TinyImageNet} \cite{le2015tinyimagenet}: 20 tasks, 10 disjoint classes per task (chance $0.1$).
\end{itemize}
Architectures follow the recorded run configurations: CIFAR-10 and the pretrained-frozen and TinyImageNet runs use ResNet-18 variants (plain, subnet, LoRA, or adapter), whereas standard and overlap-heavy CIFAR-100 use ResNet-34 variants \cite{he2016deep}. All regimes use SGD \cite{robbins1951sgd} (learning rate $0.01$, momentum $0.9$, weight decay $5\times10^{-4}$, batch size $32$) with a rehearsal budget of $500$; standard Split-CIFAR-100 LoRA uses $10^{-3}$ because $10^{-2}$ diverges. The \emph{standard} protocol (Table~\ref{tab:main_standard}) trains $20$ epochs per task, the \emph{overlap-heavy} from-scratch protocol (Table~\ref{tab:pall_overlap_heavy}) trains $3$, and the \emph{pretrained} regime (Table~\ref{tab:pretrained}) freezes an ImageNet backbone as the intended PEFT setting. Per-method hyperparameters are: PALL-Adapter bottleneck $16$, $\alpha_f{=}0.3$, $\alpha_p{=}0.2$, $10$ uniform-target and $50$ repair steps; PALL-Modified protection ratio $0.2$, $\lambda{=}1$; LoRA rank $8$; subnet sparsity $0.8$. Python, NumPy, and PyTorch RNGs are seeded, but deterministic kernels were not enabled. CIFAR uses seeds $\{0,1,2\}$ and TinyImageNet $\{0,1\}$ (MIA and anchor studies $\{0,1\}$); with these counts, cross-method gaps are descriptive rather than statistically confirmed. Baselines include ER, DER++, EWC, LwF, CLPU, LoRA, SSD, and SalUn \cite{thrun1998lifelong,buzzega2020dark,kirkpatrick2017overcoming,li2017learning,liu2022clpu,hu2021lora,foster2024ssd,fan2024salun}.

We report the final average accuracy over retained tasks $A_{\mathrm{final}}$ ($\uparrow$), the standard average forgetting $F_{\mathrm{avg}}$ ($\downarrow$), the retained-average drop at the forgetting step Fu ($\approx 0$ desired), the worst per-task retained accuracy drop
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:worstdrop}
\mathrm{WorstDrop} = \max_{t \in \mathcal{T}_{active}} \left( A_{t}^{before} - A_{t}^{after} \right)
\end{equation}
($\downarrow$), the post-forget accuracy on the forgotten task Au (ideal forgetting reaches the random-guess level), and $R_{\mathrm{upd}}$: the nominal fraction of total model parameters included in the retained-repair trainable scope ($\downarrow$). This logged scope is an efficiency proxy; it is not a count of every coordinate whose value changes during reset, classifier unlearning, and repair.

\section{Experimental Results and Analysis}

\subsection{Matched Standard Split-CIFAR Comparison}
\label{subsec:standard-protocol}
Table~\ref{tab:main_standard} is the primary comparison. It uses 20 epochs per task, three seeds, and the same standard Split-CIFAR request schedule for all eleven methods. SSD and SalUn come from their matched-protocol aggregates; the separate single-seed SSD tuning runs are not used.

CLPU is the strongest row overall, and we do not claim to outperform it. Its result follows directly from task isolation: the evaluated implementation stores one full network per active task, routes each task through its own network, and forgets by deleting that entry. The measured sub-millisecond $T_f$ therefore times a dictionary deletion, not the training or memory cost of maintaining several full networks. CLPU is attractive when that isolation is operationally acceptable; it is not a shared-model unlearning solution.

Among the shared-model methods, PALL-Modified holds $A_{\mathrm{final}}$ within half a point of CLPU ($0.9555$ vs.\ $0.9604$ on CIFAR-10; $0.7346$ vs.\ $0.7391$ on CIFAR-100), puts $A_u$ at chance ($0.5000$ and $0.0903$), and keeps WorstDrop at $0.0035$ and $0.0010$. That combination is the empirical case for the method: it comes close to the isolation reference without giving every task its own network. The remaining columns are just as telling. EWC and LwF post zero WorstDrop on CIFAR-10, but their $A_u$ sits at $0.9418$ and $0.9582$, so they preserve the task rather than forget it. SSD leaves $A_u=0.8030$ on CIFAR-10, and both SSD and SalUn stay well above the $0.1$ target on CIFAR-100. With three seeds these gaps are descriptive, not statistically confirmed.

The two daggered PEFT rows are diagnostics, not capacity-matched competitors. Under this from-scratch protocol LoRA and PALL-Adapter both sit on a randomly initialized frozen ResNet and train only small modules and the classifier. Their CIFAR-100 accuracies are nearly the same ($0.2686$ and $0.2643$) despite quite different forgetting mechanisms, and both recover once the backbone is pretrained (Table~\ref{tab:pretrained}), so the bottleneck is the representation rather than the forgetting step. We therefore evaluate PALL-Adapter in its intended pretrained-frozen setting next.

\subsection{Parameter Efficiency}
\label{subsec:efficiency}
PALL-Adapter sharply reduces the logged nominal retained-repair scope relative to the full-network variants: from $0.0240$ to $0.0030$ on CIFAR-10 (an $87.5\%$ reduction), from $0.0210$ to $0.0055$ on CIFAR-100 (${\sim}74\%$), and from $0.0960$ to $0.0292$ on TinyImageNet (${\sim}70\%$). These ratios compare the trainable repair scope, not the union of all coordinates changed across reset, shared forgetting, ascent, and repair. This smaller scope coincides with the bounded WorstDrop of Table~\ref{tab:pall_overlap_heavy} rather than trading it away.

\subsection{Overlap Composition and Temporal Behavior}
\label{subsec:overlap-analysis}
Across the adapter configurations, the measured critical-overlap ratio $|S_{share\_crit}|/|S_{forget}|$ ranges from $0.35$ to $0.56$ and the hard-protected fraction from $0.07$ to $0.11$; request-level logs show the targeted task collapsing while retained-task trajectories persist. Pooled regressions of signed WorstDrop on measured overlap have $95\%$ heteroskedasticity-robust confidence intervals that include zero, so we make no overlap--damage dose-response claim.

\subsection{Privacy Evidence: MIA, Leakage Audit, and Linear Probes}
\label{subsec:mia}
Because the shared adapter remains a function of target-buffer gradients, we assess approximate task-level forgetting with three complementary probes; none is a formal removal guarantee.

\paragraph{Membership inference.} For each request, members are up to $512$ forgotten-task training examples and non-members up to $512$ held-out test examples; the score is negative cross-entropy over maximum softmax confidence (larger $=$ more member-like) and we report its ROC-AUC. Logged AUC stays near $0.5$ (PALL-Modified: $0.5045\!\to\!0.4961$ on CIFAR-10, $0.4998\!\to\!0.5279$ on CIFAR-100); with two seeds and no significance test we read these descriptively, not as proof of indistinguishability.

\paragraph{Attack-threshold leakage audit.} Inspired by confidence-bound privacy auditing \cite{steinke2023privacy}, we threshold the stored MIA scores and report $\hat{\varepsilon}_{attack}=\max_\tau\log(\mathrm{TPR}^{L}_{95}/\mathrm{FPR}^{U}_{95})$, clipped at zero (one-sided Clopper--Pearson). Because our setup uses no randomized canaries or DP mechanism, $\hat{\varepsilon}_{attack}$ is an attack-specific leakage statistic, not a bound on the model's DP parameter. Over six request events per configuration, PALL-Adapter's post-forget mean is $0.0058$ (CIFAR-10) and $0.0000$ (CIFAR-100), while PALL-Modified averages ${\approx}0.10$ with maxima $0.4555$ and $0.5544$. A zero value certifies nothing---only that this audit found no positive leakage.

\paragraph{Linear probes on deployed representations.} Finally, we fit a fresh linear classifier on the forgotten task's full training split and evaluate it on the held-out test split, using the model's deployed penultimate features before and after each request. Full-network methods and CLPU show large probe-accuracy decreases (e.g., PALL-Modified: $-0.2168$ on CIFAR-10, $-0.1790$ on CIFAR-100), whereas PALL-Adapter and LoRA change by ${\approx}{-}0.01$. Their backbone is frozen (though the adapter pathway can change), so substantial forgotten-task information remains linearly accessible. This structural limitation complements rather than contradicts the near-chance Au and MIA results, read through the model's own decision pathway.

\subsection{Anchor Ablation: Data-Independent Protection}
\label{subsec:anchor}
The $\ell_2$ anchor of Eq.~\eqref{eq:repair} defaults to the pre-forget weights $w_{old}$, which can themselves carry forgotten-task information. A data-independent reinitialization anchor $w_{reinit}$ addresses this and performs at least as well on two seeds: $A_{\mathrm{final}}$ rises to $0.9316$ (CIFAR-10) and $0.5029$ (CIFAR-100), and the CIFAR-100 WorstDrop halves to $0.0100$. The post-forget MIA-AUC shifts are small and non-uniform, so we treat $w_{reinit}$ as directional evidence, not a privacy guarantee.

\subsection{Sensitivity and Importance-Criterion Ablations}
\label{subsec:ablation}
On pretrained CIFAR-100, an $\{\alpha_f, \alpha_p, N_{iters}\}$ sweep over eight configurations keeps $A_{\mathrm{final}}$ in the narrow band $0.8251$--$0.8289$, while increasing the forgetting steps from $10$ to $30$ lowers WorstDrop in all four pairs (best $0.0130$); $\alpha_p=0.2$ achieves this while hard-protecting only $7.7\%$ of coordinates versus $29\%$ at $\alpha_p=0.4$. Bottleneck-width sweeps showed no monotone benefit beyond dimension $16$, our fixed default. Replacing the gradient-magnitude criterion with the gradient-conflict energy $\mathrm{relu}(-g_{forget}\cdot g_{retain})$ helps the adapter on CIFAR-10 (WorstDrop $0.0160\!\to\!0.0063$) but degrades PALL-Modified on CIFAR-100 ($0.0060\!\to\!0.0230$), so gradient magnitude remains the default.

\subsection{Cross-Architecture Generalization}
\label{subsec:vit}
On a ViT-T/8 backbone, PALL-Modified reduces mean WorstDrop on Split-CIFAR-100 from $0.0190$ (PALL-Original) to $0.0100$ while slightly improving accuracy ($0.3990$ vs.\ $0.3921$) and keeping Au near chance; on Split-CIFAR-10 the two are indistinguishable across seeds. Transfer is thus dataset-dependent, with no significance claim.

\section{Limitations}
The overlap-heavy TinyImageNet comparison uses one fixed request schedule. The current interface is request-driven: it does not automatically identify what should be removed, and the requester must supply the target task ID. The task-aware setting is easier than class-incremental CL, and Au, MIA, and the leakage audit are indirect signals rather than privacy certificates. The implementation deletes the target entry from in-process rehearsal memory, but does not remove raw data, checkpoints, or external copies. Linear probes expose residual representation-level information for frozen-backbone methods. Runtime logs are descriptive and not hardware-normalized because exact server metadata was not retained. Adapter results are also sensitive to $\{\alpha_f,\alpha_p,N_{iters},p\}$; our sweeps map this sensitivity but do not select hyperparameters automatically.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented two mechanisms for selective forgetting when tasks share parameters. PALL-Modified protects the most retention-sensitive part of the shared subnetwork and comes within half a percentage point of the full-model isolation reference (CLPU) on both standard Split-CIFAR benchmarks; PALL-Adapter trades some capacity for a much smaller retained-repair scope and works best with a pretrained frozen backbone. CLPU remains preferable when a full network per task is affordable, whereas PALL targets the shared-model case. The MIA, leakage-audit, and linear-probe results keep the claim narrow: the methods suppress the requested task at the evaluated interface, not exact, certified, or storage-level data removal.
