\begin{abstract}
Continual learning systems deployed in real-world environments must often honor requests to unlearn specific information. In task-incremental settings, selective forgetting requires suppressing a designated target task while maintaining performance on all retained tasks. However, when tasks share underlying model parameters, resetting or repairing weights for one task frequently degrades others. To address this parameter overlap, we introduce EPALL, an enhanced Privacy-Aware Lifelong Learning approach. Our approach identifies shared parameter coordinates, ranks them according to their importance to retained tasks, and anchors high-sensitivity weights during the repair phase. Evaluated on Split-CIFAR benchmarks, EPALL effectively reduces forgotten-task accuracy to the chance level while staying within 0.5 percentage points of full-network baselines. Compared to the original PALL algorithm, it consistently achieves higher final accuracy and significantly reduces the maximum drop in task performance. Additionally, we analyze an alternative parameter-efficient adapter mechanism showing that unlearning in this setup is primarily driven by target-path resets. Overall, EPALL provides a robust framework for empirical selective forgetting in shared-parameter continual learning.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Continual learning (CL) systems acquire tasks sequentially while trying to prevent catastrophic forgetting \cite{mccloskey1989catastrophic,kirkpatrick2017overcoming}. Selective forgetting poses the inverse operational problem: after a task-level deletion request, the model should suppress that task without catastrophically degrading the tasks that remain active \cite{cao2015towards,bourtoule2021machine,mantelero2013forgotten}. We study this task-incremental setting and do not equate task suppression with deleting individual records, raw data, checkpoints, or external copies.

The core difficulty is \emph{parameter overlap}: a high-capacity network spreads each task's knowledge across many shared weights. Running an unconstrained unlearning step on a target task $D_f$---gradient ascent, or Fisher-based scrubbing \cite{golatkar2020eternalsunshinespotlessnet}---therefore also moves weights that the retained tasks $D_r$ depend on, and their accuracy drops as a side effect.
%
The Privacy-Aware Lifelong Learning (PALL) framework \cite{ozdenizci2025pall} modularizes task knowledge with stored subnetworks, but its request-time reset and repair do not distinguish shared coordinates by their sensitivity to retained tasks---which matters under heavy overlap, where perturbing a small but important shared region damages several active tasks. The main contributions of this paper are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Retention-sensitive shared-weight protection:} EPALL measures structural overlap between forgotten and active subnets, ranks shared coordinates by retained-task sensitivity, and anchors the critical subset during repair.
    \item \textbf{Matched evaluation and a controlled overlap benchmark:} an eleven-method, eight-seed Split-CIFAR comparison reports retention, forgetting, target accuracy, and request time jointly with paired analysis; a further $120$-run benchmark sweeps five pre-specified overlap levels across six methods.
    \item \textbf{Negative mechanism audit:} controls over a parameter-efficient adapter pathway isolate target reset, repair, soft masking, and classifier ascent and attribute its suppression to reset rather than the overlap-aware update, so a reset-driven effect is not sold as a second contribution.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Continual Learning and Replay}
Continual learning accumulates knowledge across a stream of tasks \cite{thrun1998lifelong,delange2022survey}. Regularization methods anchor parameters by estimated importance \cite{kirkpatrick2017overcoming,zenke2017continual,li2018learning,aljundi2018mas}, while rehearsal methods include ER \cite{chaudhry2019tiny}, DER++ \cite{buzzega2020dark}, and iCaRL \cite{rebuffi2017icarl}. Closest in spirit to our mechanism are the gradient-projection variants GEM and A-GEM \cite{lopezpaz2017gem,chaudhry2019agem}, which also let retained-task gradients constrain the update but project it globally rather than protecting a selected shared region. A third family isolates parameters per task via pruning \cite{mallya2018packnet}, hard attention masks \cite{serra2018hat}, or supermasks over a fixed backbone \cite{wortsman2020supsup}, exploiting the fact that trainable subnetworks exist inside dense or even random networks \cite{frankle2019lottery,ramanujan2020hidden}. PALL inherits its per-task binary masks from this line, specifically winning-subnetwork continual learning \cite{kang2022wsn}, later relaxed to soft subnetworks \cite{kang2023softnet}, which is what makes \emph{structural} overlap between task masks well defined. CLPU \cite{liu2022clpu} is the strongest isolation-based reference: each active task gets its own full network and a request deletes it, so interference is impossible by construction but storage grows with the task count. EPALL targets the complementary case, where tasks already share one model.

\subsection{Machine Unlearning Strategies}
Retraining-based methods partition data into isolated ensemble members \cite{bourtoule2021machine}, and amnesiac training stores per-batch updates for later subtraction \cite{graves2021amnesiac}. Deletion guarantees exist for convex models \cite{guo2020certified,neel2021descent,sekhari2021remember} but do not transfer to the non-convex sequential setting we study, which is why our claims stay empirical. Approximate methods instead edit gradients to scrub information \cite{golatkar2020forgetting}, and Boundary Unlearning shifts output decision boundaries for speed \cite{chen2023boundary}; in a continual setting an unconstrained update of this kind can damage feature space that active tasks still use. Saliency Unlearning (SalUn) \cite{fan2024salun} confines updates to influential weights, Selective Synaptic Dampening (SSD) \cite{foster2024ssd} dampens parameters by Fisher-based importance, and controlled distillation can jointly support learning and removal under a fixed memory \cite{chatterjee2024unified}. Closest to our setting, Learning with Selective Forgetting \cite{shibata2021lsf} erases a class via mnemonic codes while learning continues; the same coupling recurs in recoverable forgetting \cite{ye2022recoverable}, contrastive lifelong forgetting \cite{shan2024contrastive}, embedding-level class-incremental unlearning \cite{zuo2024ecilmu}, generative forgetting \cite{heng2023amnesia}, unlearning framed as reversed continual learning \cite{zhang2023reversing}, and continual unlearning for language models \cite{gao2025llmunlearn}; see \citet{nguyen2024survey} for a broader survey. Two findings bear on our design: sparsity narrows the approximate-unlearning gap \cite{jia2023sparsity}, which a subnetwork method inherits, and forget/retain gradient conflict is itself an optimization target \cite{patel2025conflicts}. UnCLe generates task-specific parameters and studies post-deletion relapse without retained data \cite{adhikari2025uncle}; CATA studies sequential removal and persistence for vision--language models via conflict-averse task-vector aggregation \cite{lin2026cata}. Our shared-model setting keeps a bounded rehearsal memory and edits overlapping weights directly, but these works motivate a separate persistence audit rather than treating immediate post-request accuracy as sufficient.

\subsection{Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning}
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT), through adapters \cite{houlsby2019parameter} and LoRA \cite{hu2021lora}, restricts training to compact modules inserted into a frozen network. The recent BID-LoRA preprint \cite{rachapudi2026bidlora} explores a related continual-learning/unlearning combination. We audit whether measuring overlap inside a shared bottleneck changes request-time endpoints beyond target-path reset and retained-task repair.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Why Unconstrained Forgetting Fails}
PALL identifies task-specific parameters, resets or reconstructs coordinates from stored masks, and repairs the retained tasks. What it does not do is separate shared coordinates by how sensitive the retained tasks are to them, so under heavy overlap reset and repair can disturb pathways several active tasks lean on. We map that intersection and protect it directly.

\subsection{Protecting Retention-Sensitive Weights}
Let $M_f$ be the binary subnetwork mask stored for the task requested for deletion and let $M_r$ be the union of the masks of the retained tasks. Their shared support is
\begin{equation}
S_{share}=M_f\cap M_r.
\end{equation}
EPALL ranks $S_{share}$ by retained-buffer gradient magnitude (or gradient-conflict energy in an ablation) and selects $S_{share\_crit}$; these binary-subnetwork sets differ from the gradient-selected adapter sets below. The target rehearsal entry is used only for request-time diagnostics, then removed from memory; no raw-data, checkpoint, or external-copy deletion is claimed. The inherited PALL reset is branch-dependent because retained backups and historical masks decide which coordinates are preserved or reconstructed. Retention repair is restricted to the nominal shared repair mask and minimizes
\begin{align}
\label{eq:repair}
\mathcal{L}_{repair} &= \alpha\,\mathcal{L}_{DER} + \beta\,\mathcal{L}_{CE} \nonumber\\
&\quad + \lambda \sum_{w \in S_{share\_crit}} (w-w_{anchor})^2 .
\end{align}
Here $\mathcal{L}_{DER}$ matches stored logits, $\mathcal{L}_{CE}$ uses retained labels, and the last term limits drift on the critical shared coordinates. The default anchor is the pre-request value $w_{old}$; we separately evaluate a fresh reinitialization anchor $w_{reinit}$ because $w_{old}$ can itself encode information about the forgotten task.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{EPALL Selective Forgetting}
\label{alg:pall-modified}
\small
\textbf{Input}: target task $f$, task masks $\{M_t\}$, rehearsal memory $\mathcal{B}$, model weights $\theta$, protection ratio $\rho$, anchor weight $\lambda$
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Copy $M_f$; if conflict scoring is selected, cache its forget gradient while $\mathcal{B}_f$ is available
\STATE Delete $\mathcal{B}_f$ and remove $M_f$ from the active mask set
\STATE $M_r \leftarrow \bigvee_{t\ne f}M_t$; $S_{share}\leftarrow M_f\cap M_r$
\STATE Compute retained-buffer sensitivity $q_i=|\nabla_i\mathcal{L}_{retain}|, \forall i\in S_{share}$
\STATE $S_{share\_crit}\leftarrow$ the top $\lceil\rho|S_{share}|\rceil$ coordinates by $q_i$
\STATE Cache anchors on $S_{share\_crit}$ and sparse retained-task backups required by the inherited reset branches
\STATE Apply PALL's branch-dependent target reset/reconstruction; derive the nominal retained-repair mask
\FOR{$k=1,\ldots,N_{repair}$}
  \STATE Sample retained rehearsal entries and minimize Eq.~\eqref{eq:repair} only on the repair mask
\ENDFOR
\STATE Rebuild the active union mask and discard request-only target state
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:pall-modified} gives the default gradient-magnitude path, and Figure~\ref{fig:epall} summarizes the problem, the selection rule, and the request-time procedure. The conflict ablation changes only line 4; the component study changes the anchor or the ranking rule, never the request schedule.

\subsection{Exploratory Adapter Pathway}
% Preserve the advisor's prose edit; do not convert these three groups back to a list.
To cut the update footprint and keep tasks from interfering, we freeze the feature-extraction layers ($\theta_{base}$). Only three groups stay trainable: a shared bottleneck adapter ($\phi_{shared}$), task-specific adapters ($\phi_{t}$), and one shared classifier used through task-specific row slices. Both adapters are standard post-backbone residual bottlenecks with ReLU \cite{houlsby2019parameter,nair2010relu}, applied in sequence (shared, then task-specific).

A request follows a strict order so that every forget-dependent gradient is estimated while the target rehearsal entry is still available. First, absolute shared-adapter gradients on the target and retained buffers define top-ratio sets $S_{forget}$ and $S_{active}$; their intersection is $S_{share\_crit}$, whose top $\lceil\alpha_p|S_{share\_crit}|\rceil$ coordinates by retained-gradient magnitude (gradient-conflict energy only in the stated ablation) form the hard-protected set $H$. Second, the target adapter is reinitialized (Kaiming down-projection \cite{he2015delving}, zero up-projection), its classifier slice reset, and the shared adapter optimized toward a uniform distribution over the target classes, each coordinate's step scaled by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:softmask}
m_{soft}(w) = \begin{cases}
0, & w \in H, \\
1-p, & w \in S_{share\_crit}\setminus H, \\
1, & w \in S_{forget}\setminus S_{active}, \\
0, & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $p\in[0,1]$. Third, the target classifier takes a gradient-ascent update from its pre-reset forget gradient, the target rehearsal entry is deleted, and all trainable parameters are briefly repaired on retained buffers.

The mask therefore governs only the shared-adapter displacement during the uniform-target subphase; it is not a permanent protection mask and does not bound end-to-end retained accuracy, since the reset, the classifier ascent, and the unmasked repair also move the endpoint. The controls below test it empirically rather than treating the multiplier as a guarantee. Since the final shared adapter remains a function of target-buffer gradients, this pathway is neither exact-unlearning nor privacy-certified; Theorem~\ref{thm:no-exact} makes that precise.

\subsection{Theoretical Delimitation}
Two statements bound what a request can deliver: no variant studied here attains \emph{exact} unlearning, which is why our claims stay empirical, and the adapter bottleneck provably shrinks the coordinate set that protection must arbitrate.

\begin{theorem}[No exact unlearning at request time]
\label{thm:no-exact}
Fix the initialization and seed. Let $\theta^{after}$ follow a request that removes task $f$, and $\theta^{retrain}$ a model trained on the retained stream alone. If either (i) some coordinate takes a step $w\leftarrow w-\eta\,m_{soft}(w)\nabla_{w}\mathcal{L}_{unlearn}(\mathcal{B}_f;w)$ with $m_{soft}(w)>0$, or (ii) some coordinate is anchored in Eq.~\eqref{eq:repair} to $w_{anchor}=w_{old}$, a value obtained by training on $\mathcal{B}_f$, then $\theta^{after}\not\perp\mathcal{B}_f$, and in general $\theta^{after}$ and $\theta^{retrain}$ differ in distribution.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under (i), unrolling $N$ steps gives $w^{(N)}=w^{(0)}-\eta\,m_{soft}(w)\sum_{i<N}\nabla_{w}\mathcal{L}^{(i)}_{unlearn}(\mathcal{B}_f)$, a deterministic function of $\mathcal{B}_f$, non-constant whenever the gradient map is. Under (ii) the anchored objective is minimized at a point displaced toward $w_{old}$, itself a measurable function of $\mathcal{B}_f$. Either way the predictive distribution depends on $\mathcal{B}_f$, whereas retraining induces one that does not.
\end{proof}

EPALL meets (ii) via its default $w_{old}$ anchor and PALL-Adapter meets (i) via the uniform-target subphase, so neither is a certified-removal mechanism; this also motivates the $w_{reinit}$ ablation, since a data-independent anchor closes route (ii) but not route (i).

\begin{lemma}[Bottleneck contraction of critical overlap]
\label{lem:bottleneck}
Let $d$ be the backbone feature dimension, $m\ll d$ the shared-adapter bottleneck width, and $\rho_{full}$ the critical-overlap fraction over the $d$ backbone coordinates. If the down-projection acts on the forget and retain gradients as a random projection $\mathbb{R}^{d}\!\to\!\mathbb{R}^{m}$, then the fraction $\rho_{ad}$ inside the bottleneck obeys $\mathbb{E}[\rho_{ad}] \le (m/d)\,\rho_{full} + \mathcal{O}(\sqrt{m\log d}/d)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By Johnson--Lindenstrauss the projection preserves the inner products defining the top-ratio sets up to $1\pm\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\log d/m})$, so top-$\alpha$ membership survives up to that distortion; only $m$ of $d$ directions survive, scaling the expected count of jointly critical coordinates by $m/d$.
\end{proof}

The bound is relative to the backbone, not inside the bottleneck: our logs put $|S_{share\_crit}|/|S_{forget}|$ at $0.35$--$0.56$, so the bottleneck contracts the arbitration problem without leaving the surviving coordinates overlap-free, matching Section~\ref{subsec:adapter-components}.

\section{Experimental Setup}

Three public benchmarks with fixed schedules interleave task training and forgetting requests, spanning increasing task count and representation difficulty: CIFAR-10 \cite{krizhevsky2009learning} as 5 two-class tasks (chance $0.5$); CIFAR-100 \cite{krizhevsky2009learning} as 10 five-class tasks in the overlap-heavy protocol (chance $0.2$) and 10-class tasks in the standard protocol (chance $0.1$); and TinyImageNet \cite{le2015tinyimagenet} as 20 ten-class tasks (chance $0.1$).
Apart from the ViT ablation, CIFAR-10, TinyImageNet, and pretrained-frozen runs use ResNet-18 variants; main CIFAR-100 runs use ResNet-34 variants \cite{he2016deep}. Configurations use SGD \cite{robbins1951sgd} (learning rate $0.01$, momentum $0.9$, weight decay $5\times10^{-4}$, batch size $32$) and a replay budget of $500$, partitioned a priori across tasks without post-deletion reallocation. Split-CIFAR-100 LoRA alone uses $10^{-3}$ because $10^{-2}$ diverges. Schedules train each task at most once, contain three forget requests, and never reintroduce a deleted task; the standard, overlap-heavy, and pretrained protocols use 20 epochs, 3 epochs, and a frozen ImageNet backbone. PALL-Adapter uses task/shared bottlenecks $16$, $\alpha_f{=}0.3$, $\alpha_p{=}0.2$, $p=|S_{share\_crit}|/|S_{forget}|$, 10 forget steps, and 50 repair steps. EPALL uses protection ratio $0.2$ and $\lambda{=}1$; LoRA rank is 8; subnet sparsity is $0.8$ on CIFAR-10/TinyImageNet and $0.9$ on CIFAR-100. Reruns used one RTX 3070 Ti (8\,GB), dual Xeon E5-2620 v4 CPUs, 31\,GB RAM, Ubuntu 24.04.4, Python 3.12.3, PyTorch 2.5.1, and deterministic scripts. Seeds are $\{0,\dots,7\}$ for the standard comparison, $\{0,\dots,5\}$ for the direct EPALL controls, $\{0,1,2\}$ for the pretrained-PEFT, overlap-heavy, adapter-component, and corrected-MIA analyses, $\{0,1\}$ for the anchor, persistence, and retraining references, and one matched schedule for storage. Small-sample results are descriptive unless an exact paired test is reported.

We report retained-task $A_{\mathrm{final}}$ ($\uparrow$). For CL context, $F_{\mathrm{avg}}(r)=|\mathcal{T}_{trained}|^{-1}\sum_t[\max_{k<r}A_t(k)-A_t(r)]$ covers every task trained by request $r$, including later deletions; request-specific claims use retained-only Fu and WorstDrop plus forgotten-task Au. Fu is the retained mean before minus after a request, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:worstdrop}
\mathrm{WorstDrop} = \max_{t \in \mathcal{T}_{active}} \left( A_{t}^{before} - A_{t}^{after} \right)
\end{equation}
($\downarrow$) is signed, not clipped. Au is measured immediately and read through $|\mathrm{Au}-\mathrm{chance}|$. Tables use each run's final forget event, then mean and sample standard deviation across seeds. $R_{\mathrm{upd}}$ is the nominal retained-repair trainable fraction only, not total compute, storage, memory, runtime, or changed coordinates.

\section{Experimental Results and Analysis}

\subsection{Standard Split-CIFAR Comparison}
\label{subsec:standard-protocol}
Table~\ref{tab:main_standard} is the primary comparison: 20 epochs per task, eight seeds, and within each seed the same standard Split-CIFAR request schedule for all eleven methods. SSD and SalUn come from matched-protocol aggregates; the single-seed SSD tuning runs are unused.

CLPU is the strongest row overall and we do not claim to outperform it: it stores one full network per active task and forgets by deleting that entry, so its sub-millisecond $T_f$ times a dictionary deletion, not the cost of maintaining those networks. It is attractive when isolation is acceptable, but it is not a shared-model solution. Among the shared-model methods, EPALL reaches $A_{\mathrm{final}}=0.9433$ on CIFAR-10 against CLPU's $0.9500$, and on CIFAR-100 it matches CLPU within seed noise ($0.7371$ vs.\ $0.7355$). It puts $A_u$ near chance ($0.4863$ and $0.0959$) and keeps WorstDrop at $0.0027$ and $0.0056$, reaching the isolation reference without a network per task; we read the CIFAR-100 ordering as a tie, not a win, since the gap is far inside the seed spread. Other columns matter too: EWC and LwF post zero WorstDrop on CIFAR-10 but leave $A_u$ at $0.8122$ and $0.9469$, retaining much target-task behavior, while SSD leaves $A_u=0.8453$ there and both SSD and SalUn stay well above the $0.1$ target on CIFAR-100 ($0.2619$, $0.3967$).

Since schedules are paired by seed we also compare EPALL with PALL-Original directly. On CIFAR-100 $\Delta A_{\mathrm{final}}$ is positive in seven of eight pairs (mean $+0.0147$, descriptive bootstrap $[0.0062,0.0229]$), with mean gains of $0.0110$ in $F_{\mathrm{avg}}$ and $0.0166$ in WorstDrop. On CIFAR-10 the accuracy gain is $+0.0087$ $[0.0034,0.0151]$ and the WorstDrop gain $0.0092$ $[0.0029,0.0157]$, while $F_{\mathrm{avg}}$ is mixed ($-0.0017$ $[-0.0078,0.0033]$). Retention costs request time: EPALL is about $2.10$\,s and $12.06$\,s slower than PALL-Original on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100.

With eight pre-fixed seeds, exact one-sided Wilcoxon tests reach $p=0.0078$ on CIFAR-100 for $A_{\mathrm{final}}$, $F_{\mathrm{avg}}$, and WorstDrop (seven of eight pairs favour EPALL; $d_z=1.11$, $1.35$, $1.51$). On CIFAR-10 both $A_{\mathrm{final}}$ ($p=0.0078$, seven of eight pairs, one tie, $d_z=0.96$) and WorstDrop ($p=0.03125$) are now significant, so the retention advantage holds on both datasets; CIFAR-10 $F_{\mathrm{avg}}$ still shows no consistent improvement.

The two daggered PEFT rows are diagnostics, not capacity-matched competitors: here LoRA and PALL-Adapter both sit on a randomly initialized frozen ResNet. Their CIFAR-100 accuracies are similarly low ($0.2695$ and $0.2583$) despite different forgetting mechanisms, and both recover once the backbone is pretrained (Table~\ref{tab:pretrained}), pointing to a representation bottleneck rather than the forgetting step.

\subsection{Adapter Component Attribution}
\label{subsec:adapter-components}
Five matched arms isolate the request-time components under the corrected pretrained pipeline (three seeds): reset-only reinitializes the target adapter and classifier slice; reset+repair adds retained repair; uniform drops overlap protection; mask/no-ascent drops the cached classifier ascent; full uses everything.

The RNG-neutral endpoints do not support the soft mask as the source of the adapter's behavior: full and uniform differ by at most $0.0006$ in $A_{\mathrm{final}}$ or WorstDrop, and full and mask/no-ascent are identical. The cached classifier ascent is the one component with a measurable, if small, effect: removing it leaves retention untouched but moves the forgotten task from chance by $0.0040$ $[0.0020,0.0060]$ on CIFAR-100 and $0.0017$ $[0.0000,0.0030]$ on CIFAR-10. On CIFAR-100 reset-only is in fact better: retention $0.8324$ vs.\ $0.8244$, WorstDrop $0$ vs.\ $0.0147$, and a smaller mean distance to chance. Paired bootstrap intervals over three schedules include zero for the full--reset accuracy and Au-distance differences, while the WorstDrop difference favors reset-only. We therefore attribute the suppression primarily to target-path reset; the overlap-aware shared update remains empirically unsupported at the measured endpoint.

On CIFAR-100, PALL-Original's WorstDrop rises from $0.0130$ to $0.0390$ as overlap grows while EPALL stays within $0.0060$--$0.0190$; CIFAR-10 keeps the ordering over a smaller range. SalUn degrades like PALL-Original, CLPU stays at zero by construction. This is a level-wise trend: realized overlap differs across methods, and pooled regressions on measured overlap mostly include zero (the exception is the adapter's CIFAR-100 slope, $0.0398$ $[0.0190,0.0606]$).

\paragraph{Overlap composition and persistence.} Adapter logs put $|S_{share\_crit}|/|S_{forget}|$ at $0.35$--$0.56$ and the hard-protected fraction at $0.07$--$0.11$. Across two-seed histories, worst rebounds are $0.0490$/$0.0160$ for PALL-Adapter and $0.0185$/$0.0480$ for EPALL on CIFAR-10/100: suppression is persistent but not invariant. With no post-deletion training, this does not test renewed-learning relapse as in UnCLe or CATA.

\paragraph{Anchor ablation.}
The $\ell_2$ anchor of Eq.~\eqref{eq:repair} defaults to $w_{old}$, which by Theorem~\ref{thm:no-exact}(ii) carries forgotten-task information. Replacing it with $w_{reinit}$ (two seeds) moves $A_{\mathrm{final}}$ from $0.9291$ to $0.9316$ and WorstDrop from $0.0045$ to $0.0037$ on CIFAR-10, and $0.4956$ to $0.5029$ and $0.0200$ to $0.0100$ on CIFAR-100 --- directional evidence, not a privacy guarantee.

\paragraph{Sensitivity and importance criterion.}
The legacy-normalized pretrained sweep is excluded. A two-seed CIFAR-10 width sweep over $\{4,8,16,32,64,128\}$ is non-monotone, so $16$ is a compact default, not a claimed optimum. Gradient-conflict scoring helps the adapter on CIFAR-10 (WorstDrop $0.0160\!\to\!0.0063$) but hurts EPALL on CIFAR-100 ($0.0060\!\to\!0.0230$), so gradient magnitude stays the default; optimizing the conflict \cite{patel2025conflicts} rather than scoring by it is a natural next step.

\paragraph{Cross-architecture transfer.} On a ViT-T/8 backbone \cite{dosovitskiy2021vit} EPALL lowers mean WorstDrop on Split-CIFAR-100 from $0.0190$ to $0.0100$ while improving accuracy ($0.3990$ vs.\ $0.3921$) and keeping Au near chance; on Split-CIFAR-10 the means are nearly equal. Transfer is dataset-dependent, with no significance claim.

\section{Limitations}
The interface requires a target task ID and does not cover class-incremental inference. Adapter controls find no soft-mask benefit beyond target reset, and classifier ascent changes $A_u$ only slightly. Direct EPALL controls separate most clearly on CIFAR-100, and the CIFAR-10 no-anchor contrast does not separate at six seeds; main-table $F_{\mathrm{avg}}$ remains non-significant on CIFAR-10. Theorem~\ref{thm:no-exact} rules out an exact-removal reading a priori and the measurements agree: corrected augmentation-matched MIA \cite{shokri2017membership,yeom2018privacy} stays near chance (CIFAR-10: $0.5006{\pm}0.0205\to0.4959{\pm}0.0155$; CIFAR-100: $0.5015{\pm}0.0169\to0.4998{\pm}0.0033$), though a low-power null is not removal evidence \cite{goel2023adversarial,hayes2024inexact} and stronger attacks \cite{carlini2022membership} or formal audits \cite{steinke2023privacy} are needed; unlearning can even raise exposure \cite{chen2021jeopardizes}. Retraining-reference agreement is $0.60$--$0.96$ on retained tasks but $0.00$--$0.98$ on the forgotten one. Only the in-process rehearsal entry is deleted, not raw data, checkpoints, or external copies; runtime is not hardware-normalized and adapter tuning is manual.

\section{Conclusion}
EPALL protects retention-sensitive shared weights, matches CLPU on Split-CIFAR-100 and comes within $0.7$ points on CIFAR-10, and holds its advantage over PALL-Original as overlap grows. Controls attribute PALL-Adapter's endpoint to target-path reset rather than its soft mask, so it stays an audited exploratory variant. CLPU is preferable when a network per task is affordable, though its resident state is $1.6$--$2.2\times$ EPALL's; per Theorem~\ref{thm:no-exact}, no result here establishes exact, certified, or storage-level removal.
