\begin{abstract}
Like other deployed AI systems, continual learning systems may receive privacy-driven requests to remove previously learned information. In the task-incremental setting, this motivates selective forgetting: suppressing a specified task while preserving performance on tasks that remain active. The problem is especially difficult when tasks share parameters, because changing weights associated with the requested task can also damage retained tasks. We introduce two extensions of Privacy-Aware Lifelong Learning (PALL). \textbf{PALL-Modified} ranks shared coordinates by retained-task sensitivity and anchors them during repair. \textbf{PALL-Adapter} places task-specific and shared bottleneck adapters over a frozen backbone and combines target-path reset with a soft-masked shared update and retained repair. For a fixed shared-adapter gradient sequence, we bound the mask's first-order contribution on the soft-protected overlap. CLPU is the strongest matched Split-CIFAR reference but stores a full network per active task; among shared-model methods, PALL-Modified stays within $0.5$ points of its final accuracy while moving forgotten-task accuracy to or near chance. Three-seed component controls show that PALL-Adapter's endpoint behavior is reset-dominated: neither the soft mask nor cached classifier ascent improves the measured endpoint over matched controls. We therefore treat the adapter as an exploratory parameter-efficient pathway, not evidence for an independently effective overlap-aware update. Neither method provides exact, certified, representation-level, or storage-level removal.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Continual learning (CL) systems acquire tasks sequentially while trying to prevent catastrophic forgetting \cite{mccloskey1989catastrophic,kirkpatrick2017overcoming}. Selective forgetting poses the inverse operational problem: after a task-level deletion request, the model should suppress that task without catastrophically degrading the tasks that remain active \cite{cao2015towards,bourtoule2021machine}. We study this task-incremental setting and do not equate task suppression with deleting individual records, raw data, checkpoints, or external copies.

The core difficulty is \emph{parameter overlap}: a high-capacity network spreads each task's knowledge across many shared weights. Running an unconstrained unlearning step on a target task $D_f$---gradient ascent, or Fisher-based scrubbing \cite{golatkar2020forgetting}---therefore also moves weights that the retained tasks $D_r$ depend on, and their accuracy drops as a side effect.

The original Privacy-Aware Lifelong Learning (PALL) framework \cite{ozdenizci2025pall} modularizes task knowledge with stored subnetworks. Its request-time reset and retained-task repair, however, do not distinguish shared coordinates by their sensitivity to retained tasks. This matters under heavy overlap, where perturbing a small but important shared region can damage multiple active tasks.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We make three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{PALL-Modified:} it measures the \emph{critical overlap} between the forgotten and the active parameters and protects it with an anchored penalty during retention repair.
    \item \textbf{PALL-Adapter:} a parameter-efficient design that freezes the backbone and routes learning through task-specific bottleneck adapters and one shared adapter, so a forget request changes adapters and classifier rows rather than the backbone.
    \item \textbf{Mechanism analysis and controls:} we bound the soft-masked subphase for a fixed gradient sequence, then isolate reset, repair, masking, and classifier ascent with paired three-seed controls. The controls do not support an endpoint benefit from the mask or ascent, delimiting the adapter claim rather than attributing reset-driven suppression to overlap awareness.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Continual Learning and Memory Replay}
Continual learning accumulates knowledge across a stream of tasks \cite{thrun1998lifelong}. Regularization methods such as Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) \cite{kirkpatrick2017overcoming} and Learning without Forgetting (LwF) \cite{li2017learning} anchor parameters, while Experience Replay (ER) and Dark Experience Replay (DER++) \cite{buzzega2020dark} keep an episodic memory buffer. CLPU \cite{liu2022clpu} is the strongest isolation-based reference here: in the implementation we evaluate, each active task gets its own full network and a request is served by deleting that network. Interference is then impossible by construction, but storage grows with the number of active tasks. PALL targets the complementary case, where tasks already share one model and cannot be separated by discarding a task network.

\subsection{Machine Unlearning Strategies}
Retraining-based methods such as SISA \cite{bourtoule2021machine} partition the data into isolated ensemble members. Approximate methods instead edit gradients to scrub information \cite{golatkar2020forgetting}, and Boundary Unlearning shifts output decision boundaries for speed \cite{chen2023boundary}. In a continual setting, though, an unconstrained update can damage feature space that active tasks still use. Saliency Unlearning (SalUn) \cite{fan2024salun} confines updates to influential weights; Selective Synaptic Dampening (SSD) \cite{foster2024ssd} dampens parameters by Fisher-based importance. Controlled distillation can jointly support learning and removal with a fixed memory \cite{chatterjee2024unified}. UnCLe instead generates task-specific parameters and explicitly studies post-deletion task relapse without retained data \cite{adhikari2026uncle}; CATA studies sequential removal and persistence for vision--language models through conflict-averse task-vector aggregation \cite{lin2026cata}. Our task-incremental shared-model setting keeps a bounded rehearsal memory and edits overlapping weights directly, but these works motivate a separate persistence audit rather than treating immediate post-request accuracy as sufficient.

\subsection{Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning}
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT), through adapters \cite{houlsby2019parameter} and LoRA \cite{hu2021lora}, restricts training to compact modules inserted into a frozen network. The recent BID-LoRA preprint \cite{rachapudi2026bidlora} explores a related continual-learning/unlearning combination. PALL-Adapter instead measures overlap inside a shared bottleneck and controls how that bottleneck moves at request time.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{The Limitation of Unconstrained Forgetting}
PALL identifies task-specific parameters, resets or reconstructs coordinates from stored masks, and repairs the retained tasks. What it does not do is separate shared coordinates by how sensitive the retained tasks are to them. Under heavy overlap, reset and repair can therefore disturb pathways that several active tasks lean on. We map that intersection and protect it directly.

\subsection{Proposed Method 1: PALL-Modified}
Let $M_f$ be the binary subnetwork mask stored for the task requested for deletion and let $M_r$ be the union of the masks of the retained tasks. Their shared support is
\begin{equation}
S_{share}=M_f\cap M_r.
\end{equation}
PALL-Modified ranks $S_{share}$ by retained-buffer gradient magnitude (or gradient-conflict energy in an ablation) and selects $S_{share\_crit}$. These binary-subnetwork sets differ from the gradient-selected adapter sets below. The target rehearsal entry is used only during request-time diagnostics and is then removed from in-process memory; no raw-data, checkpoint, or external-copy deletion is claimed. The inherited PALL reset is branch-dependent because retained backups and historical masks determine which coordinates are preserved or reconstructed. Retention repair is restricted to the nominal shared repair mask and minimizes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:repair}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{repair} ={}& \alpha\,\mathcal{L}_{DER} + \beta\,\mathcal{L}_{CE}\\
&+ \lambda \sum_{w \in S_{share\_crit}} (w-w_{anchor})^2 .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal{L}_{DER}$ matches stored logits, $\mathcal{L}_{CE}$ uses retained labels, and the final term limits drift on the selected critical shared coordinates. The default anchor is the pre-request value $w_{old}$; we separately evaluate a fresh reinitialization anchor $w_{reinit}$ because $w_{old}$ can itself encode information about the forgotten task.

\subsection{Proposed Method 2: PALL-Adapter}
To cut the update footprint and keep tasks from interfering, we freeze the feature-extraction layers ($\theta_{base}$). Only three groups stay trainable:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Shared bottleneck adapter} ($\phi_{shared}$),
    \item \textbf{Task-specific adapters} ($\phi_{t}$),
    \item \textbf{One shared classifier, used through task-specific row slices.}
\end{enumerate}

For a backbone feature $x$, the shared and task-specific post-backbone residual adapters with ReLU \cite{nair2010relu} compute
\begin{align}
z_{shared} &= x + \mathbf{W}_{up}^{(s)} \cdot \mathrm{ReLU}\left(\mathbf{W}_{down}^{(s)} \cdot x\right), \\
z_{final} &= z_{shared} + \mathbf{W}_{up}^{(t)} \cdot \mathrm{ReLU}\left(\mathbf{W}_{down}^{(t)} \cdot z_{shared}\right).
\end{align}

\subsection{Ordered Soft-Masked Unlearning}
The implementation uses a strict order so that every forget-dependent gradient is estimated while the target rehearsal entry is still available. First, absolute shared-adapter gradients on the target and retained buffers define top-ratio sets $S_{forget}$ and $S_{active}$ and their intersection $S_{share\_crit}$. Within this intersection, $H$ contains the top $\lceil\alpha_p|S_{share\_crit}|\rceil$ coordinates by retained-gradient magnitude (gradient-conflict energy only in the stated ablation) and is hard protected. Second, the target task adapter is reinitialized (Kaiming down-projection and zero up-projection) and its classifier slice is reset. The shared adapter is then optimized toward a uniform target:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{unlearn} = \mathbb{E}_{x \in D_{forget}} \Big[ -\tfrac{1}{|C_f|}\sum_{c\in C_f} \log p(c\mid x; \Phi) \Big],
\end{equation}
where $C_f$ is the target task's class set and $\Phi=(\phi_{shared},\phi_{t_{forget}},C_{t_{forget}})$ denotes the deployed target-task path; the implementation scores only that classifier slice.
Each coordinate's update is then scaled by a \emph{soft-scaling} multiplier $m_{soft}$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:softupdate}
w^{(i+1)} = w^{(i)} - \eta \cdot m_{soft}(w) \cdot \nabla_{w} \mathcal{L}_{unlearn},
\end{equation}
where $m_{soft}(w)$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:softmask}
m_{soft}(w) = \begin{cases}
0, & \text{if } w \in H, \\
(1-p), & \text{if } w \in S_{share\_crit}\setminus H, \\
1, & \text{if } w \in S_{forget}\setminus S_{active}, \\
0.0, & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $p\in[0,1]$. Third, the target classifier receives a gradient-ascent update computed from its pre-reset forget gradient, the target rehearsal entry is deleted, and all trainable adapter and classifier parameters are briefly repaired on retained-task buffers. Thus the soft mask governs the uniform-target shared-adapter subphase; it is not a permanent protection mask over the entire request.

\begin{algorithm}[tb]
\caption{Ordered PALL-Adapter Forgetting}
\label{alg:unlearning}
\textbf{Input}: target task $t_{forget}$, active tasks $\mathcal{T}_{active}$, shared adapter $\phi_{shared}$, task adapter $\phi_{t_{forget}}$, target slice $C_{t_{forget}}$ of the shared classifier\\
\textbf{Parameter}: $\alpha_f = 0.3$, $\alpha_p = 0.2$, learning rate $\eta$, protection strength $p$
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Compute forget and retained shared-adapter gradients from their rehearsal entries
\STATE $S_{forget} \leftarrow$ top-$\alpha_f$ coords of $|\nabla \mathcal{L}_{forget}|$; $S_{active} \leftarrow$ top-$\alpha_p$ coords of $|\nabla \mathcal{L}_{active}|$
\STATE $S_{share\_crit} \leftarrow S_{forget} \cap S_{active}$; \quad $S_{forget\_only} \leftarrow S_{forget} \setminus S_{active}$
\STATE $H\leftarrow$ top-$\alpha_p$ fraction of $S_{share\_crit}$ by retained-gradient score; reset $\phi_{t_{forget}}$ and classifier slice $C_{t_{forget}}$
\FOR{iteration $= 1, \dots, N_{iters}$}
    \STATE sample batch $(x, y) \in D_{t_{forget}}$; compute $\mathcal{L}_{unlearn}$ against the uniform target
    \STATE $\nabla \phi_{shared} \leftarrow \mathrm{backward}(\mathcal{L}_{unlearn})$
    \FORALL{$w \in \phi_{shared}$}
        \STATE $m_{soft} \leftarrow 0$ on $H$; $(1-p)$ on $S_{share\_crit}\setminus H$; $1$ on $S_{forget\_only}$; else $0$
        \STATE $w \leftarrow w - \eta \cdot m_{soft} \cdot \nabla_{w}$
    \ENDFOR
\ENDFOR
\STATE Apply classifier ascent from the cached forget gradient; delete the target rehearsal entry
\STATE Repair retained tasks for $N_{repair}$ steps on their rehearsal entries
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Mechanism Scope}
The multiplier in Eq.~\eqref{eq:softmask} directly scales the shared-adapter displacement during the uniform-target subphase, but it does not by itself bound end-to-end retained accuracy: the target adapter reset, classifier ascent, and unmasked retained repair also affect the final model. We therefore treat $(1-p)$ as an implementation-level damping factor and evaluate its practical effect through measured WorstDrop. Because the final shared adapter remains a function of target-buffer gradients, neither method is an exact-unlearning or privacy-certified mechanism.

\subsection{Contraction of the Shared-Adapter Subphase}
\label{subsec:theory}
We now formalize the single component the soft mask directly controls. Consider the uniform-target subphase (Algorithm~\ref{alg:unlearning}, lines~5--10), during which $\theta_{base}$ and every task adapter are frozen and only $\phi_{shared}\in\mathbb{R}^d$ is updated by Eq.~\eqref{eq:softupdate} for $N$ steps. Let $\phi^{(0)}$ be its pre-subphase value and $\Delta_t^{\mathrm{sub}}\triangleq R_t(\phi^{(N)})-R_t(\phi^{(0)})$ the change this subphase \emph{alone} induces in a retained task's risk $R_t$; the reset, classifier ascent, and unmasked repair are separate, which is why this is not an end-to-end bound (cf.\ Mechanism Scope). Partition the coordinates into $\mathcal{F}^{\!\circ}=S_{forget}\setminus S_{active}$ ($m_i{=}1$), $\mathcal{S}=S_{share\_crit}\setminus H$ ($m_i{=}1{-}p$), and the frozen remainder ($m_i{=}0$). We assume (i) each $R_t$ is $\beta$-smooth; (ii) $|g_i^{(k)}|\le G$, so $\Gamma_i\triangleq|\sum_{k}g_i^{(k)}|\le NG$; and (iii) $|\partial_i R_t(\phi^{(0)})|\le\epsilon$ on $\mathcal{F}^{\!\circ}$. Assumption~(iii) is the tail-gradient assumption motivating the top-$K$ retained-buffer estimate of $S_{active}$; the finite buffer does not guarantee it for population risk.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-gradient critical-overlap bound]
\label{thm:contraction}
Let $E_t^{\mathrm{soft}}=\eta\!\sum_{i\in \mathcal{S}}\!|\partial_i R_t(\phi^{(0)})|\,\Gamma_i$, $E_t^{H}=\eta\!\sum_{i\in H}\!|\partial_i R_t(\phi^{(0)})|\,\Gamma_i$, and $C_t=\eta\!\sum_{i\in\mathcal{F}^{\!\circ}}\!\Gamma_i$. Under (i)--(iii), the soft-masked subphase satisfies
\begin{equation*}
\Delta_t^{\mathrm{sub}}\!\le\!(1{-}p)E_t^{\mathrm{soft}}\!+\!\epsilon C_t\!+\!\tfrac{\beta}{2}\eta^2N^2G^2\big(|\mathcal{F}^{\!\circ}|\!+\!(1{-}p)^2|\mathcal{S}|\big).
\end{equation*}
For the \emph{same fixed sequence} $\{g^{(k)}\}_{k=1}^N$, replacing the mask by $m_i{=}1$ on $S_{forget}$ gives a first-order term $E_t^{\mathrm{soft}}+E_t^{H}+\epsilon C_t$. Thus the mask scales the soft part by $(1-p)$ and removes the $H$ contribution. This is not a comparison with a separately rerun unconstrained trajectory, whose later gradients can differ.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Summing Eq.~\eqref{eq:softupdate}, $\delta_i=-\eta m_i\sum_k g_i^{(k)}$, so $|\delta_i|\le\eta m_i\Gamma_i$. The descent lemma gives $\Delta_t^{\mathrm{sub}}\le\langle\nabla R_t,\delta\rangle+\tfrac{\beta}{2}\|\delta\|_2^2$; the $m_i{=}0$ coordinates vanish, $\mathcal{F}^{\!\circ}$ contributes $\le\epsilon C_t$ by~(iii), and $\mathcal{S}$ contributes $\le(1{-}p)E_t^{\mathrm{soft}}$. The stated curvature term follows from $m_i^2\in\{1,(1{-}p)^2,0\}$. For the same $g_i^{(k)}$, setting every coordinate in $S_{forget}$ to multiplier one restores the $E_t^{\mathrm{soft}}+E_t^H$ first-order contribution.
\end{proof}

\section{Experimental Setup}

We evaluate task-level continual forgetting over three public benchmarks with fixed schedules that interleave task-training and forgetting requests. The benchmarks span a small coarse-grained problem, a larger fine-grained problem, and a higher-resolution 200-class problem, allowing us to test increasing task count and representation difficulty:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{CIFAR-10} \cite{krizhevsky2009learning}: 5 sequential tasks, 2 disjoint classes per task (random-guess forgotten-task accuracy $0.5$).
    \item \textbf{CIFAR-100} \cite{krizhevsky2009learning}: 10 sequential tasks, 5 disjoint classes per task in the overlap-heavy protocol (chance $0.2$), and 10-class tasks in the standard protocol (chance $0.1$).
    \item \textbf{TinyImageNet} \cite{le2015tinyimagenet}: 20 tasks, 10 disjoint classes per task (chance $0.1$).
\end{itemize}
Apart from the ViT ablation, CIFAR-10, TinyImageNet, and pretrained-frozen runs use ResNet-18 variants; main CIFAR-100 runs use ResNet-34 variants \cite{he2016deep}. Configurations use SGD \cite{robbins1951sgd} (learning rate $0.01$, momentum $0.9$, weight decay $5\times10^{-4}$, batch size $32$) and a replay budget of $500$, partitioned a priori across tasks without post-deletion reallocation. Split-CIFAR-100 LoRA alone uses $10^{-3}$ because $10^{-2}$ diverges. Main schedules train each task at most once, contain three distinct forget requests, and never reintroduce a deleted task. Standard, overlap-heavy, and pretrained protocols respectively use 20 epochs, 3 epochs, and a frozen ImageNet backbone. PALL-Adapter uses task/shared bottlenecks $16$, $\alpha_f{=}0.3$, $\alpha_p{=}0.2$, $p=|S_{share\_crit}|/|S_{forget}|$, 10 forget steps, and 50 repair steps. PALL-Modified uses protection ratio $0.2$ and $\lambda{=}1$; LoRA rank is 8; subnet sparsity is $0.8$ on CIFAR-10/TinyImageNet and $0.9$ on CIFAR-100. Current reruns used one NVIDIA RTX 3070 Ti (8 GB), dual Intel Xeon E5-2620 v4 CPUs, 31 GB RAM, Ubuntu 24.04.4, Python 3.12.3, PyTorch 2.5.1, and torchvision 0.20.1. Current scripts are deterministic. Legacy TinyImageNet overlap-heavy rows instead use $k{=}1$, one fixed schedule, non-deterministic execution, and---for the adapter---one-step ascent. The standard Split-CIFAR, corrected pretrained PEFT, overlap-heavy PALL, and component-control tables use seeds $\{0,1,2\}$. The anchor and persistence analyses use $\{0,1\}$; the excluded privacy diagnostics also remain incomplete. These counts support descriptive, not confirmed, rankings.

We report retained-task $A_{\mathrm{final}}$ ($\uparrow$). For CL context, $F_{\mathrm{avg}}(r)=|\mathcal{T}_{trained}|^{-1}\sum_t[\max_{k<r}A_t(k)-A_t(r)]$ covers every task trained by request $r$, including later deletions; request-specific claims use retained-only Fu and WorstDrop plus forgotten-task Au. Fu is retained mean before minus after a request, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:worstdrop}
\mathrm{WorstDrop} = \max_{t \in \mathcal{T}_{active}} \left( A_{t}^{before} - A_{t}^{after} \right)
\end{equation}
($\downarrow$) is signed, not clipped. Au is measured immediately and read through $|\mathrm{Au}-\mathrm{chance}|$. Tables use each run's final forget event, then mean and sample standard deviation across seeds. $R_{\mathrm{upd}}$ is only the nominal retained-repair trainable fraction, not total compute, storage, memory, runtime, or changed coordinates.

\section{Experimental Results and Analysis}

\subsection{Matched Standard Split-CIFAR Comparison}
\label{subsec:standard-protocol}
Table~\ref{tab:main_standard} is the primary comparison. It uses 20 epochs per task and three seeds; within each seed, all eleven methods receive the same standard Split-CIFAR request schedule. SSD and SalUn come from their matched-protocol aggregates; the separate single-seed SSD tuning runs are not used.

CLPU is the strongest row overall, and we do not claim to outperform it. Its result follows directly from task isolation: the evaluated implementation stores one full network per active task, routes each task through its own network, and forgets by deleting that entry. The measured sub-millisecond $T_f$ therefore times a dictionary deletion, not the training or memory cost of maintaining several full networks. CLPU is attractive when that isolation is operationally acceptable; it is not a shared-model unlearning solution.

Among the shared-model methods, PALL-Modified holds $A_{\mathrm{final}}$ within half a point of CLPU ($0.9555$ vs.\ $0.9604$ on CIFAR-10; $0.7346$ vs.\ $0.7391$ on CIFAR-100), puts $A_u$ at or near chance ($0.5000$ and $0.0903$), and keeps WorstDrop at $0.0035$ and $0.0010$. That combination is the empirical case for the method: it comes close to the isolation reference without giving every task its own network. The remaining columns are just as telling. EWC and LwF post zero WorstDrop on CIFAR-10, but their $A_u$ sits at $0.9418$ and $0.9582$, so they preserve the task rather than forget it. SSD leaves $A_u=0.8030$ on CIFAR-10, and both SSD and SalUn stay well above the $0.1$ target on CIFAR-100. With three seeds these gaps are descriptive, not statistically confirmed.

The two daggered PEFT rows are diagnostics, not capacity-matched competitors. Under this from-scratch protocol LoRA and PALL-Adapter both sit on a randomly initialized frozen ResNet and train only small modules and the classifier. Their CIFAR-100 accuracies are nearly the same ($0.2686$ and $0.2643$) despite quite different forgetting mechanisms, and both recover once the backbone is pretrained (Table~\ref{tab:pretrained}); this pattern is consistent with a representation bottleneck rather than isolating the forgetting step as the cause. We therefore evaluate PALL-Adapter in its intended pretrained-frozen setting next.

\label{subsec:pretrained}

The endpoints do not support the soft mask or cached ascent as the source of Adapter's behavior. Full and mask/no-ascent have identical $A_{\mathrm{final}}$ and WorstDrop on both datasets, while full and uniform differ by at most $0.0002$ in accuracy and $0.0007$ in WorstDrop. On CIFAR-100, reset-only instead has higher retention ($0.8324$ vs. $0.8286$), zero WorstDrop, and a smaller mean distance to chance than full. Paired bootstrap intervals over three schedules include zero for the full--reset accuracy and Au-distance differences, whereas the WorstDrop difference favors reset-only. We therefore attribute the observed task suppression primarily to target-path reset; the overlap-aware shared update remains a theoretically delimited but empirically unsupported component at the measured endpoint.

\subsection{Nominal Retained-Repair Scope}
\label{subsec:efficiency}
PALL-Adapter reduces the logged nominal retained-repair scope relative to the full-network variants: from $0.0240$ to $0.0030$ on CIFAR-10 (an $87.5\%$ reduction), from $0.0210$ to $0.0055$ on CIFAR-100 (${\sim}74\%$), and from $0.0960$ to $0.0292$ on TinyImageNet (${\sim}70\%$). These ratios cover only the trainable repair scope. They do not establish lower end-to-end compute, storage, peak memory, wall-clock time, or total parameter changes; task-specific adapter storage also grows with the number of tasks. Table~\ref{tab:pall_overlap_heavy} reports measured WorstDrop alongside the scope proxy, which is not covered by the subphase theorem.

\subsection{Overlap Composition and Temporal Behavior}
\label{subsec:overlap-analysis}
Across the current uniform-loop adapter aggregates with overlap logs, the measured critical-overlap ratio $|S_{share\_crit}|/|S_{forget}|$ ranges from $0.35$ to $0.56$ and the hard-protected fraction from $0.07$ to $0.11$; request-level logs show forgotten-task accuracy moving toward chance while retained-task trajectories remain measurable. Pooled regressions of signed WorstDrop on measured overlap have $95\%$ heteroskedasticity-robust confidence intervals that include zero, so we make no overlap--damage dose-response claim.

\paragraph{Persistence across later deletion requests.} A read-only audit follows each deleted task through the remaining request history in matched two-seed runs (six deleted-task trajectories per method and dataset). PALL-Adapter's mean maximum rebound is $0.0093$ on CIFAR-10 and $0.0027$ on CIFAR-100, with worst observed rebounds $0.0490$ and $0.0160$; PALL-Modified's corresponding worst rebounds are $0.0185$ and $0.0480$. These descriptive trajectories show that immediate suppression generally persists through later deletions but is not invariant. Because the schedules contain no subsequent training request after deletion begins, they do not test relapse under renewed learning as in UnCLe or CATA.

\subsection{Privacy Evidence: MIA, Leakage Audit, and Linear Probes}
\label{subsec:mia}
Because the shared adapter remains a function of target-buffer gradients, privacy diagnostics require particular care; none can be treated as a formal removal guarantee.

\paragraph{Audit protocol.} The original diagnostic used training-time augmentation for members but deterministic preprocessing for non-members, and its rank-based AUC did not average tied scores. We therefore omit those numerical MIA, threshold-leakage, and probe results rather than interpret a confounded audit. The corrected implementation evaluates both splits with the same augmentation-free transform and average-ranks ties; corrected reruns are required before these diagnostics are restored. Even after correction, a near-chance attack is only a diagnostic null and not a privacy or removal certificate \cite{steinke2023privacy}.

\subsection{Anchor Ablation: Data-Independent Protection}
\label{subsec:anchor}
The $\ell_2$ anchor of Eq.~\eqref{eq:repair} defaults to the pre-forget weights $w_{old}$, which can themselves carry forgotten-task information. In the matched two-seed anchor ablation, replacing it with $w_{reinit}$ changes $A_{\mathrm{final}}$ from $0.9291$ to $0.9316$ and WorstDrop from $0.0045$ to $0.0037$ on CIFAR-10; on CIFAR-100 the corresponding changes are $0.4956$ to $0.5029$ and $0.0200$ to $0.0100$. This is directional retention evidence, not a privacy guarantee.

\subsection{Sensitivity and Importance-Criterion Ablations}
\label{subsec:ablation}
The earlier pretrained $\{\alpha_f,\alpha_p,N_{iters}\}$ sweep used the legacy CIFAR-normalization pipeline and is excluded from the corrected endpoint evidence. Bottleneck-width sweeps showed no monotone benefit beyond dimension $16$, our fixed default. Replacing the gradient-magnitude criterion with the gradient-conflict energy $\mathrm{relu}(-g_{forget}\cdot g_{retain})$ helps the adapter on CIFAR-10 (WorstDrop $0.0160\!\to\!0.0063$) but degrades PALL-Modified on CIFAR-100 ($0.0060\!\to\!0.0230$), so gradient magnitude remains the default.

\subsection{Cross-Architecture Generalization}
\label{subsec:vit}
On a ViT-T/8 backbone, PALL-Modified reduces mean WorstDrop on Split-CIFAR-100 from $0.0190$ (PALL-Original) to $0.0100$ while slightly improving accuracy ($0.3990$ vs.\ $0.3921$) and keeping Au near chance; on Split-CIFAR-10 their mean results are nearly equal. Transfer is thus dataset-dependent, with no significance claim.

\section{Limitations}
TinyImageNet overlap-heavy results use one fixed schedule and legacy settings (Table~\ref{tab:pall_overlap_heavy}). The request-driven, task-aware interface requires a target task ID and does not cover class-incremental inference. Three-seed component controls find no endpoint benefit from PALL-Adapter's soft mask or classifier ascent beyond target-path reset; the adapter mechanism is therefore exploratory, not a validated second improvement over PALL. The distributional retraining reference and corrected privacy audits are pending, and Au is not a privacy certificate. Only the in-process rehearsal entry is deleted, not raw data, checkpoints, or external copies, and a frozen backbone can retain target information. Runtime is not hardware-normalized. Adapter tuning is not automatic.

\section{Conclusion}
PALL-Modified protects retention-sensitive shared weights and comes within half a point of CLPU on both standard Split-CIFAR benchmarks. PALL-Adapter provides a compact, task-ID-aware pathway, but component controls show that its measured endpoint is dominated by target-path reset rather than the proposed soft mask or cached ascent. We therefore make PALL-Modified the supported positive result and retain Adapter as an audited exploratory variant. CLPU remains preferable when a full network per task is affordable; PALL targets shared models. No result establishes exact, certified, representation-level, or storage-level removal.
